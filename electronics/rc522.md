# RC522 NFC reader → Raspberry Pi 4

Board: Raspberry Pi 4 Model B (2 GB) — logic level: **3.3 V** — powered from:
the Pi's 3.3 V rail (pin 1), which comes from the official 5 V 3 A USB-C supply.

The RC522 is a 3.3 V part. **Never connect it to a 5 V pin**; that can damage
the reader. It draws about 13–26 mA, well within the Pi's 3.3 V rail.

It talks SPI, the Pi's fast 4-wire bus: clock (SCK), data out (MOSI), data in
(MISO), and a chip-select line (SDA on the RC522 board, CE0 on the Pi) that
says "this message is for you". The LCD will join the same bus later on the
second chip-select (CE1, pin 26), which is why the reader takes CE0.

| Signal | Pi pin (physical) | Pi GPIO | RC522 pin | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.3 V power | 1 | — | 3.3V | **Not 5 V** |
| Ground | 20 | — | GND | Any GND pin works; 20 is next to the SPI pins |
| SPI chip select | 24 | GPIO 8 (CE0) | SDA | Labelled SDA/SS/NSS on most boards |
| SPI clock | 23 | GPIO 11 (SCLK) | SCK | |
| SPI data Pi → reader | 19 | GPIO 10 (MOSI) | MOSI | |
| SPI data reader → Pi | 21 | GPIO 9 (MISO) | MISO | |
| Reset | 22 | GPIO 25 | RST | High = running; the daemon pulses it low to reset a stuck reader |
| Interrupt | — | — | IRQ | Leave unconnected (the software polls) |

Physical pin numbers count from pin 1 (the corner nearest the SD card, marked
by the square pad), odd pins on the inner row, even pins on the outer edge.

## Pins kept free for later

From `product.md` (Hardware): GPIO 3 (pin 5) is reserved for the power button
(it can wake a halted Pi), CE1 / GPIO 7 (pin 26) for the LCD's chip select.
The composite video and audio use the 3.5 mm jack, not GPIO.

## Checking it

With dupont jumpers (female–female), wire as above, then on the Pi:

```bash
systemctl --user stop lasertrack.service
~/lasertrack/.venv/bin/lasertrack nfc-test
systemctl --user start lasertrack.service
```

`nfc-test` prints `RC522 found, version 0x92` (or 0x91, 0x88, 0xB2 on clones)
and then `tag_inserted 04:…` when a tag is held near it. A version of `0x00`
or `0xFF` means no reply on SPI: check the wiring, the 3.3 V supply, and that
SPI is enabled (`ls /dev/spidev0.0`).
